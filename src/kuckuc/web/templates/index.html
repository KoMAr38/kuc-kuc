“Kuc-kuc” is Serbian for “knock-knock”

A knock on the door when dangerous weather comes.

My grandmother lives alone in a village in southern Serbia. She has a landline, no smartphone, and a neighbour across the road.

When a heatwave or a hard frost is forecast, Kuc-kuc phones her and asks if she is all right. If she does not answer, or says she is not well, it phones the neighbour and asks him to knock on her door. I am far away, and I see all of it as it happens.

How it works

  1. The forecast crosses a line

    Every three hours the forecast for her village is checked: heat that feels like 35 °C or more, frost of −10 °C or lower, gusts over 80 km/h, or 20 cm of snow in a day.

  2. Her phone rings

    A calm voice, in her language, says what is coming and asks: are you all right? She just answers out loud. No app, no smartphone, no buttons to press. Her few seconds of answer are understood by Gemini.

  3. A neighbour knocks

    No answer after three tries, an unclear answer, or “I am not well”: the neighbour's phone rings. Twenty minutes later he is asked how she is. The family gets a message at every step.

One rule decides everything: any doubt counts as a problem. Silence, a wrong number, a failed call or a confused answer never becomes “she is fine”.

Try it on your phone

You play the grandmother. Kuc-kuc calls your phone with the English version of the same call and shows, live, what it does with your answer. Answer “I'm fine”, say you feel unwell, or do not pick up at all. If you do not pick up, it tries twice more and then calls you again, this time as the neighbour. (For the demo the waits are seconds. For a real person they are ten and twenty minutes.)

How to get the code

The code proves the number is yours, so nobody can make this call anyone else. The demo calls numbers in the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom and Norway, at most twice a day per number. Your number is deleted within two days, and the recording of your answer after seven days. Twilio, the phone company we use, keeps its own record of the call.
